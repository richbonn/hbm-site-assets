Refinance

Refinancing without waiting for the tax return to catch up.

A refinance can change your payment, your term, or your cash position. It can also cost more than it saves. Run the break-even first.

Why people refinance

  • To lower the monthly payment.
  • To shorten the term and pay interest for fewer years.
  • To take cash out of the property for a business, an investment, or another goal.
  • To move out of a loan that no longer fits how the income actually works.

The tradeoffs

Closing costs are real. Starting a new 30-year clock can lower the payment and still raise total interest. Break-even is the closing costs divided by the monthly savings. If you may move or refinance again before that point, the math may not work.

Self-employed borrowers can sometimes document income for a refinance with bank statements. Whether that may be an option depends on your file.

Questions we hear

How do I know if refinancing is worth it?

Compare the monthly savings to the closing costs, then compare total cost over the time you expect to keep the loan. The calculator above does both. We do not guarantee savings.

Can I roll closing costs into the loan?

Often you can, which raises the loan amount. That can protect cash now and cost more over time. The calculator lets you test both.

Will you tell me not to refinance?

Yes, when the numbers say so.
