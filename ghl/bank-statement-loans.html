Bank statement mortgages

Your deposits tell the story. We read them.

A bank statement mortgage works out your income from the money that actually came into your accounts, not only from what your tax return shows after write-offs. It is the only thing this site does, so we try to do it well.

How income gets figured

Same mortgage. A different way of reading you.

A standard loan expects your income to show up neatly on a tax return or a pay stub. If you run a business or get paid in uneven chunks, that checklist can read you wrong. A bank statement loan lets the lender use the documents that show what actually happened.

  1. Add up the deposits

    The lender totals deposits over a set period. Many programs use 12 or 24 months.

  2. Remove what is not income

    Transfers between your own accounts, loan proceeds, refunds. If it did not come from the business, it does not count.

  3. Apply an expense factor

    For business accounts, the program treats a share of deposits as business expenses. A common expense factor is 50%, but it varies by program, and some programs use personal statements instead.

  4. Divide by the months

    What is left, divided by the number of months, gives a monthly figure a lender can evaluate. Try it in the income illustrator.

Program rules differ by lender, and details depend on your file. Nothing here says you qualify.

Who it fits

It may be an option if this sounds like you.

  • You own a business and your tax return shows far less than the business takes in.
  • You are a freelancer or contractor and clients pay into an account you control.
  • Your income swings. Seasonal work, big clients, strong and slow quarters.
  • Your return lags the business. You grew fast, and last year’s paperwork has not caught up.
  • A lender already said no because the return did not match the life you are living.

If you have a regular paycheck and a clean tax return, this probably is not the right tool, and we will tell you that early.

What the lender looks at

The documents. The pattern underwriters want to see.

  • 12 or 24 consecutive months of statements, every page.
  • Proof you are self-employed, such as a business license or a CPA letter. Many programs want 2 or more years.
  • Government-issued ID.
  • Personal statements too, in some cases, depending on whether the program uses business or personal statements.

Underwriters want consistent deposits, a clear line between business and personal money, no overdrafts, and no big unexplained transfers. Requirements vary by lender and program, and we confirm them against your file.

What can derail it

The usual ways a file goes sideways.

  • NSF or overdraft fees in the last 12 months. Even one can flag the file, so clean up the account history before applying, not during
  • Personal and business money mixed in one account
  • Large deposits nobody can explain
  • Gaps in the statement history
  • Moving money between accounts without telling us
  • New debt, or a change of business structure, mid-process

Most of these are fixable early and painful late. Tell us before you do any of them.

Already told no?

A lender’s no is an answer. It is not always the final answer.

Take the denial seriously, and not as a verdict. Then ask what the problem actually was: the borrower, the document, the program, the sequence, or the timing. A no based on a tax return is worth a second look with the bank statements next to it.

Questions we hear

The skeptical ones, answered.

Is this safe?

Bank statement loans get a bad rap from the stated-income era. These programs still document your income from real deposits, and the lender still has to evaluate whether you can repay. Terms vary by lender and program, and we explain the ones that apply to you.

Do I need to send tax returns?

Bank statement programs exist for people whose returns do not show the full picture, so many do not use returns to calculate income. Documentation requirements vary by program, and we confirm them against your file.

Business or personal statements?

Some programs use business statements, some use personal, some allow either. We tell you which fits your file.

Are the rates higher?

Often, yes. We show you the actual numbers before you decide, and results vary.

Do I need a big down payment?

It depends on the program, the property, and your file. We tell you before you write an offer.

How long does it take?

Timelines depend on the file and the lender. We tell you what we expect, then update you on a call every week.

Can I move to a conventional loan later?

Some borrowers do once their documentation changes. We make no promises, and it is worth asking us up front.
